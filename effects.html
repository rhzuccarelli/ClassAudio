colour &
saturation

Iron, tape and valves change a sound even when you do not ask them to. This page shows what each one does to the wave and to its harmonics, and why "warm" is something you can measure.

1

three kinds of change

Every effect on this page falls into one of three families. Knowing the family tells you in advance what will happen to the spectrum.

linear

Only turns things up or down

Filters and EQ. They change the level of the harmonics that are already there. They never create a new frequency.

eq · filter · the tone knob
non-linear

Creates harmonics

Overdrive, transformers, tape saturation. The output is a bent copy of the input, and bending a wave adds harmonics. With two notes it also adds sum and difference tones.

drive · iron · tape · valves
time-varying

Moves things around

Chorus, flanger, tape wow and flutter. The effect changes over time, so each partial is smeared or split into sidebands, like the FM lab in the guide.

chorus · flanger · vibrato · wow
Real gear is usually all three at once. A tape machine saturates (non-linear), loses treble (linear) and wobbles in speed (time-varying). The labs let you hear each part separately.
Each lab shows three screens: the wave (grey is the input, white is the output), the device curve in the middle, and the spectrum (hollow grey bars are the input, white is the output).
2

overdrive & clipping

An overdrive is a curve. Every input value is looked up on the curve and replaced by the output value. The middle screen shows the curve. The orange stretch is the part of it your signal actually uses. While the signal stays on the straight middle part, nothing happens. Push it into the bends and harmonics appear.

2.1soft, hard, and the shape of the knee

Soft clipping (a smooth curve, like a valve or a tape) adds harmonics gradually, and they fall off quickly with number. Hard clipping (a transistor or an op-amp hitting its rails) flattens the top of the wave suddenly. The sharp corner sprays harmonics far up the spectrum, which sounds harsh and buzzy.

2.2even and odd: symmetry

A curve that treats positive and negative the same way can only add odd harmonics (3, 5, 7…), as in a square wave. Turn up asymmetry and the curve bends more on one side. That adds even harmonics (2, 4, 6…), which sit an octave and an octave-plus-fifth above the note and blend with it. This is a big part of why single-ended valve stages are called "warm". The fuzz curve is asymmetric by design.

2.3chords: intermodulation

Switch the source to two tones. A distorted pair of notes produces not only harmonics of each note, but also sum and difference tones: f2 − f1, 2f1 − f2 and more. With a fifth (3:2) those products land on the note one octave below the root, which still sounds consonant. That is why guitarists play power chords. With a major third (5:4) they land two octaves below the root and in between, which sounds muddy.

2.4aliasing

Hard clipping creates harmonics above 24 kHz, higher than a 48 kHz digital system can store. They fold back down as aliases: clean-looking lines that are not on the harmonic grid. Set hard, drive up, source saw at a high pitch, and compare 1× with 4× oversampling. Good plugins process at a higher rate internally for exactly this reason.

3

transformers

An audio transformer is two coils on an iron core. The audio becomes magnetism in the iron and back again. Iron can only hold so much magnetism. Past that point it saturates, and the output stops following the input. The middle screen plots the core's magnetisation against the magnetic drive: its B–H curve.

3.1low notes saturate first

Unlike an overdrive, a transformer's distortion depends on frequency. The magnetism builds up over each half cycle, so a slow bass wave pushes the core much further than a fast treble wave at the same level. Keep the level fixed and sweep the pitch down. The harmonics grow as the note drops. This is the "weight" and "thickness" that people hear on bass through vintage consoles like the Neve 1073 or the Pultec, while the top end stays clean.

3.2hysteresis and dc

Iron also "remembers" which way it was magnetised. The B–H curve opens into a loop, called hysteresis. That adds a little distortion even at low levels. A DC bias current, found in single-ended valve amplifiers where the valve current flows through the output transformer, shifts the loop off-centre. That adds even harmonics, just like asymmetry in the overdrive.

Set pitch to 40 Hz, level to +6 dB, and switch core from large to small. Small cheap transformers saturate much earlier. This is part of why a guitar amp's output transformer is part of its sound.
4

tape

Magnetic tape is the whole family in one box. Each part can be switched off to hear it alone:

  • Saturation with hysteresis (non-linear). The tape particles saturate softly and remember their direction. The loop in the middle screen is the tape's transfer curve. It rounds peaks gently and adds mostly odd harmonics.
  • Head bump and treble loss (linear). Playback heads boost a band of low frequencies, and the tape loses highs. Both depend on tape speed in inches per second (ips). Slower tape means an earlier treble roll-off.
  • Wow and flutter (time-varying). The motor and capstan are never perfectly steady. Slow speed drift (wow, ~0.5 Hz) and fast flutter (~7 Hz) bend the pitch up and down. On the spectrum this is FM: small sidebands appear right next to each partial.
  • Hiss. The random magnetic grain of the tape. A noise floor under everything.
Pick zoom ×2 on the spectrum and turn up wow & flutter. The single line at the fundamental grows skirts of sidebands 7 Hz apart. That is flutter, drawn as FM.
5

chorus & flanger

A chorus mixes the sound with copies of itself that are delayed by a few milliseconds, where the delay keeps sweeping up and down (middle screen). A changing delay is a changing pitch, like a moving car horn. Each copy is detuned slightly and the copies drift against the original, like several players on the same part.

5.1no new harmonics, but every line gets wider

A chorus is linear, so it cannot create harmonics. The spectrum shows something else: each partial is smeared into a small cluster, and the level of each one moves up and down as the copies beat against the original. On the wave screen the shape keeps changing while the pitch stays.

5.2the flanger is the same circuit

Make the delay very short (around 1 ms) and feed some output back in, and the chorus becomes a flanger. A short delay mixed with the original cancels a whole series of frequencies at once. This is a comb filter, and its notches sweep through the harmonics. Use the flanger preset with the saw source to hear the jet-plane sweep.

The Rhodes Suitcase had a stereo tremolo, not a chorus. The chorus most people link with the Rhodes came later, from pedals like the Boss CE-1 and the Roland Jazz Chorus amp. Chapter 6 lets you add one.
6

the rhodes chain

A classic recorded Rhodes goes through a preamp, an output or console transformer, a tape machine and often a chorus. Here all four stages run in order on a short e-piano phrase, or on your own recording. Switch stages on and off and compare the spectra: the hollow grey bars show the sound before the chain.

Load a dry Rhodes recording, then open the Sound Microscope in another tab. Measure the dry file, then measure the same file after you record the output of this chain. The odd/even balance, harmonic energy and decay numbers will all move.
The built-in phrase is generated by code (a simple tine-and-pickup model), not recorded. Your own file is processed in the browser and never uploaded. Only the first 8 seconds are used.

termmeaning
THD+NTotal harmonic distortion plus noise. Everything in the output that is not the input tone(s), as a percentage of the tone.
transfer curveThe graph of output against input for a non-linear device. A straight diagonal line means no distortion.
hysteresisThe output depends on where the input came from, not only where it is. It draws a loop instead of a line.
intermodulationSum and difference tones created when two or more notes pass through a non-linear device together.
aliasingFrequencies above half the sample rate folding back down as unrelated tones.
wow / flutterSlow / fast speed variations of a tape or record, heard as pitch wobble.
