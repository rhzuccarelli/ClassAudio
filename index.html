Why strings give whole-number harmonics and bells do not, and how oscillators, additive synthesis, FM and filters shape a sound. Every change shows up at once in the wave and in its Fourier spectrum.

02suggested lesson plan

One class per row. Each "try this" works as a live demo on the projector.

classpageideatry this
1Waves & Harmonics · ch. 1–4Every sound is a set of sine waves. Strings give whole-number harmonics, bars and drums do not. Timbre is the recipe.Vibrating objects lab: play ideal string, then in-between. Additive lab: pick saw, press build, watch the ramp appear one harmonic at a time.
2Waves & Harmonics · ch. 5–6Two ways to get harmonics: create them with FM, or carve them with a filter.Filter lab: saw, resonance ~15 dB, sweep cutoff. Then FM at ratio 1.41 with sweep index.
3Sound MicroscopeReal instruments are more than a recipe: every harmonic has its own life.Record one Rhodes or piano key soft and hard. Compare the harmonic decay charts and the resynthesis.
4Colour & Saturation"Warm" is measurable: even harmonics, frequency-dependent saturation, flutter.Overdrive lab: two tones, fifth vs. third. Transformer lab: sweep the pitch down at a fixed level.
5Digital Sound · ch. 1–5Sound becomes numbers: how often (sample rate) and how precisely (bits). Too few of either can be heard.Sampling lab: sine at 8k, press sweep and hear it fold back. Bit depth lab: fading note at 6 bits, then switch dither on.
6Digital Sound · ch. 6–7Every effect is multiply, add and remember. MIDI is instructions, not audio.DSP lab: echo on clicks, then shorten the delay into a comb. MIDI lab: play a chord and read the bytes.

03before class

volumeEvery page has a volume slider in its top bar. Start low on classroom speakers. Resonant filters and distortion can jump in level.
recordingsFor the microscope, use dry WAV or FLAC files of single notes. Reverb, MP3 and chords make the measurements less reliable.
projectorAll displays are drawn on dark screens that read well from the back of the room. The page follows the viewer's light or dark setting.
