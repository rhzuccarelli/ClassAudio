ClassAudio

A working reference for sound.

ClassAudio explains how sound is made, measured, coloured and stored. It is organised like a reference book: four volumes of short chapters, and an index of terms at the back.

Look a word up, read the definition, and follow the link to the chapter. Every chapter has a small lab where you can see the idea on a graph and hear it at the same time.

Runs in any modern browser. Nothing to install, and files you open stay on your computer.

Using it in class

One possible order, one class per line. Each idea has a lab you can run live on the projector. Keep the volume low at first: every page has a volume slider in its top bar, and resonant filters and distortion can jump in level.

  1. Every sound is a set of sine waves. Vol. I, ch. 1–4Play the ideal string, then the in-between one. In the additive lab, pick saw and press build.
  2. Make harmonics, or carve them away. Vol. I, ch. 5–6Filter lab: saw, resonance around 15 dB, sweep the cutoff. Then FM at ratio 1.41, sweep the index.
  3. Every harmonic of a real instrument has its own life. Vol. IIRecord one note soft and loud, and compare the harmonic decay and the resynthesis. Dry WAV or FLAC works best.
  4. “Warm” can be measured. Vol. IIIOverdrive lab: two tones, a fifth against a third. Transformer lab: sweep the pitch down at a fixed level.
  5. How often and how precisely. Vol. IV, ch. 1–5Sampling lab at 8 kHz, press sweep. Bit-depth lab: a fading note at 6 bits, then dither on.
  6. Effects are arithmetic; MIDI is instructions. Vol. IV, ch. 6–8Echo on clicks, then shorten it into a comb. Play a chord and read the bytes. Open one of your own .wav files.
