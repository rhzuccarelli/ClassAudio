In class

A possible order, one class per row. Start with the volume low: resonant filters and distortion can jump in level.

1Every sound is a set of sine wavesI · 1–4Play the ideal string, then the in-between one. In the additive lab, pick saw and press build.
2Make harmonics, or carve them awayI · 5–6Filter lab: saw, resonance around 15 dB, sweep the cutoff. Then FM at ratio 1.41, sweep the index.
3Each harmonic has its own lifeIIRecord one note soft and loud and compare the harmonic decay. Dry WAV or FLAC works best.
4“Warm” can be measuredIIIOverdrive lab: two tones, a fifth against a third. Transformer lab: sweep the pitch down at a fixed level.
5How often, and how preciselyIV · 1–5Sampling lab at 8 kHz, press sweep. Bit-depth lab: a fading note at 6 bits, then dither on.
6Effects are arithmetic; MIDI is instructionsIV · 6–8Echo on clicks, then shorten it into a comb. Play a chord and read the bytes. Open one of your own .wav files.
